[color=#ffffff][size=5][b]Context Music Manager[/b][/size][/color]

Your own music, chosen by what you're doing. CMM watches your situation — fighting, driving, swimming, standing in a trader after dark, hiding in an infested POI — and plays from the matching folder. Drop files in, they play. Nothing to restart, no playlists to build, no menus.

[b][color=#ff0000]Alpha 20.7, Alpha 21.2 and 2.6 have not been thoroughly tested[/color][/b]

[b]A music pack can be its own mod.[/b] CMM reads music from your [i]MusicPath[/i] folder [i]and[/i] the [i]Music[/i] folder of every installed mod, so a pack installs like any other mod, survives updating CMM, and works with MO2/Vortex/Wabbajack. The [i]cmm roots[/i] console command shows which folders are in use and which mod each came from.

If you put your own pack together — your own composition, or royalty-free and AI-generated content you're legally able to distribute — I'll link to it here.

[color=#ffffff][size=5][b]Music in the world[/b][/size][/color]

In-game radio objects emit music from tracks stored in [i]/Music/radio[/i] (or whichever path you define) as real positional, diegetic sound layered on top of your normal soundtrack — walk toward a speaker and it gets louder, pans across your ears, muffles behind walls, and picks up room reverb. A shared "station" keeps broadcasting even while you're out of range, so you rejoin mid-song when you come back.
[list]
[*][b]Speaker character[/b] — the sound is squeezed into a small driver in a small box, with the boxy resonance a cheap cone actually has. Ham radios are voiced harder still. (Considering a DJ system like Fallout 3/4 — maybe in 1.0.6)[/*]
[*][b]Doors matter[/b] — an open door is air and a closed one is solid, damaged doors leak sound, and sliding doors muffle gradually as they travel. Occlusion follows how much of the doorway is actually open, so a garage door and a closet door behave consistently, both leaves of a double door count, and tilted doors like the wood cellar hatch are measured properly. Standing in a doorway that has swung open sounds like standing in it, not behind it. (I'm not sure how this behaves for doors other players control.)[/*]
[*][b]Live tuning[/b] — the whole spatial feel (range, falloff, occlusion, reverb) is hot-tunable from [i]radio.xml[/i] while the game runs[/*]
[*][b]Volume[/b] — world speakers follow the game's [b]Ambient[/b] slider rather than Music, with their own [i]WorldEmitterVolume[/i] trim (default [b]0.5[/b])[/*]
[/list]

[color=#ffffff][b]A radio you carry[/b][/color]

[b]Alt+PgUp / Alt+PgDn[/b] dials through Off → station → station → Off. It works on every supported game version, including the alphas that have no speaker blocks at all. Turning it on holds your place in whatever context music was playing, and turning it off drops you back exactly where you left it, however long you listened.

[color=#ffffff][size=5][b]The audio engine[/b][/size][/color]

Nearly the entire audio pipeline lives outside the game's managed (C#/Unity) environment, in a purpose-built native C library. The music doesn't compete with the game for the main thread and isn't interrupted by garbage-collection pauses — the biggest source of stutters.
[list]
[*]Decoding, mixing and output all run in the mod's own engine ([i]CMM_Decoder.dll[/i]) on threads the mod owns, completely outside the frame loop. CMM doesn't use Unity's audio system at all[/*]
[*][b]Crossfades don't get clobbered.[/b] The mixer holds up to [b]four simultaneous voices[/b], so a new track can start while three older ones finish fading naturally. Ambient → tension → combat in two seconds sounds like one continuous handoff instead of an abrupt cut. If transitions pile up past the limit it drops only the [i]quietest[/i] voice, already nearly silent at the tail of its fade — the incoming track is never the one sacrificed[/*]
[*][b]Every format decoded natively[/b] — ogg, opus, flac, mp3, wav, m4a/aac, and the audio inside mp4/m4v/mkv/webm containers[/*]
[*][b]Per-track loudness normalization (EBU R128)[/b] — measured once per track and cached, with a manual per-track trim on a keybind[/*]
[*][b]Music survives loading freezes[/b] — tracks keep playing, and even advance, while the game's main thread is frozen during world load[/*]
[*][b]Device changes are handled[/b] — unplugging a headset or switching the default output no longer strands the music[/*]
[*][b]Gapless[/b] — the next track is decoded in advance[/*]
[*]Background work — loudness scanning, pre-loading, folder and config watching — is fully off the game loop, and libraries of 100,000+ files scan without hitching[/*]
[*]The game's [b]Overall Volume[/b] slider is applied by hand, since CMM's audio leaves through its own output device and the master slider never reached it[/*]
[/list]

[size=5][b]How the music is chosen[/b][/size]

CMM keeps a [b]stack of everything currently true about your situation[/b] (in combat, in a desert, at night, near a trader…) and plays the [b]highest-priority[/b] one that has music. Equal priorities never interrupt each other, so whatever is already playing keeps going until something genuinely more important happens.

If a context's folder is [b]empty[/b] it doesn't go silent — it follows that context's [b]fallback[/b] to the next-best choice. A few shared rules:
[list]
[*][b]Warmup[/b] - some contexts must stay active for a few seconds before they take over, so a quick dip in water or a duck into a doorway doesn't interrupt your music[/*]
[*][b]WarmGroup[/b] - a shared tag for contexts that hand off to each other, like a day/night pair. Members share one warmup timer so swapping between them stays seamless instead of briefly dropping to lower-priority music. Each context still keeps its own Warmup — set grouped members equal for the smoothest result. Used by Home and Home_Night, and by the infested / non-infested POI tiers[/*]
[*][b]Cooldown[/b] - combat and event music wait a few seconds before they can re-trigger, preventing rapid flicker[/*]
[*][b]Resume[/b] - most contexts pick up where they left off when you re-enter them; short events restart fresh[/*]
[*][b]Stale[/b] - a context is only eligible to resume from its saved position until it times out. It can be disabled per context so a context never expires while inactive[/*]
[*][b]Once[/b] - event stingers play to completion, then hand back to whatever was playing before[/*]
[*][b]Fade length follows the context.[/b] Anything that comes and goes quickly — Tension, every Combat tier, POI_Infested and its tiers, the online-player contexts — fades in fast (1.2 s) so you actually hear it before it's gone. Music tied to where you are or what time it is keeps its slow fade[/*]
[/list]

[b]Attributes[/b] you can customize per context in [i]contexts.xml[/i]:
[list]
[*][b]Name[/b] - the context's ID[/*]
[*][b]Source[/b] - its subfolder, or a .m3u/.m3u8 playlist file. A folder containing playlists also picks up their tracks alongside the loose files, with no duplicates, and edits to a playlist take effect within seconds — including on USB, network and other non-standard drives[/*]
[*][b]Subfolders[/b] - if enabled, plays everything in the folder's subfolders too[/*]
[*][b]Fallback[/b] - where it goes when it has no files: another context, the Stack, Vanilla, or Silence[/*]
[*][b]FadeInTime[/b] - how long it takes to fade in when activated[/*]
[*][b]Priority[/b] - its priority while on the stack[/*]
[*][b]Loop[/b] - one track is chosen and repeats until the context goes stale (unused by default, but the option is there)[/*]
[*][b]Bypass[/b] - circumvents the cooldown of the track currently playing[/*]
[/list]

[b]Settings[/b] in [i]settings.xml[/i] worth mentioning:
[list]
[*][b]VolumeModifier[/b] - how loud CMM tracks play relative to the game's music slider (0.0–1.0, default 0.5)[/*]
[*][b]MusicPath[/b] - the music root. Relative or absolute, so your files can live outside the mod directory or on another drive[/*]
[*][b]AddonMusicScanEnabled[/b] - whether music packs installed as separate mods are read[/*]
[*][b]TrackNoRepeatMemory[/b] - how much of a folder must play before a track can repeat (-1 = the whole folder)[/*]
[*][b]FavouriteWeight[/b] - how much more often a favourited track plays[/*]
[*][b]DisabledVanillaSections[/b] - turn off individual vanilla Dynamic Music sections (Exploration, Suspense, Combat, Bloodmoon, Trader) rather than all or nothing[/*]
[*][b]EnemyDetectRange[/b] - max distance (game units) at which an enemy counts toward combat/tension[/*]
[*][b]HomeDetectRange[/b] - distance (game units) from your bedroll or land claim that counts as "home"[/*]
[*][b]PlayerHealthDangerThresholdPercent[/b] - health fraction (0.0–1.0) below which combat escalates to Low_Health_Panic[/*]

[b]Settings[/b] in [i]radio.xml[/i] — the radio and world-speaker half, split out so it doesn't bury the rest:
[list]
[*][b]WorldEmitterEnabled / WorldEmitterVolume / WorldEmitterRange[/b] - world speakers on/off, their own volume trim (they follow the [b]Ambient[/b] slider, not Music), and how far they carry[/*]

[size=5][b]FAQ & Troubleshooting[/b][/size]

[b]Q: Do I have to fill every folder?[/b]
No. Fill only the ones you care about — empty folders fall back automatically, and ultimately to the game's vanilla music. You can start with just [i]ambient[/i] and [i]combat[/i].

[b]Q: How do I set up radio stations?[/b]
Make a subfolder inside [i]Music/radio/[/i] for each station; the folder name is the station name. The personal radio (Alt+PgUp / Alt+PgDn) and any speaker block in the world both use them.

[b]Q: Do I need speakers in the world to use the radio?[/b]
No. The personal radio works on every supported version, including the alphas that have no speaker blocks at all.

[b]Q: Does the addon work on MacOS or Linux?[/b]
Not yet. The native audio engine currently ships as a Windows library, and Windows is the only tested and supported platform. Linux support is planned for 1.0.6.

[b]Q: What audio formats are supported?[/b]
MP3, OGG (Vorbis), Opus, FLAC, WAV/AIFF, and AAC — including [i].m4a[/i] / [i].mp4[/i], and even the audio track of an MP4/MKV/WebM video.

[b]Q: Can I really change music without restarting?[/b]
Yes. Add, remove or replace files while the game runs — CMM detects the change within a few seconds, even for the track currently playing.

[b]Q: I added a file but nothing happened.[/b]
[list]
[*]Make sure it's in the correct folder and a supported format[/*]
[*]That situation may not be active yet (a [i]blood_moon[/i] track only plays on horde night)[/*]
[*]A higher-priority situation may be overriding it (combat beats ambient, and so on)[/*]
[*]Corrupt files are auto-rejected — re-export the track if it won't play[/*]
[*]If it's a music pack installed as its own mod, [i]cmm roots[/i] will tell you whether CMM can see it[/*]
[*]The coverage panel (Alt+Num8) shows exactly which contexts have music and which are falling back[/*]
[/list]
[b]Q: Multiple tracks in one folder — how are they picked?[/b]
Every track plays before any track repeats. Each context deals from a shuffled bag and reshuffles once it runs out. Favourites get extra turns and scale to the size of the folder; banned tracks are skipped. [b]Next/Prev[/b] walk your play history in that context, like a music player, rather than rolling a fresh random track. You can switch to Sequential or pure Random in [i]Config/settings.xml[/i] — only shuffled-bag has been thoroughly tested.

[b]Q: It's too loud / too quiet.[/b]
CMM follows your in-game Music volume slider, plus a [b][i]VolumeModifier[/i][/b] in [i]Config/settings.xml[/i] (default 0.5, matching the game's normal music level). World speakers follow the [b]Ambient[/b] slider instead, with their own [i]WorldEmitterVolume[/i] trim (default 0.5). Save the file and the change loads immediately if the game is running. Individual tracks are loudness-matched automatically, and [b]Alt+Up/Down[/b] saves a per-track trim on top.

[b]Q: Does this replace the vanilla music entirely?[/b]
Only where you provide tracks. Empty folders hand off to vanilla Dynamic Music, so the two coexist — and you can disable individual vanilla sections rather than all of them.

[b]Q: I left an area and came back — why did the music restart / not restart?[/b]
Tracks resume where you left off if you return quickly; after a context goes "stale" (configurable per context, and disableable) CMM picks a fresh track instead.

[b]Q: Can I store my music outside the mod folder?[/b]
Yes — set [i]MusicPath[/i] in [i]Config/settings.xml[/i] to any folder, even on another drive. You can also install music as a separate mod entirely.

[b]Q: Will updating CMM wipe my settings?[/b]
No. The config files are created on first run rather than shipped in the download, so an update only appends settings that are genuinely new and leaves your choices alone.

[b]Q: Does it work with the game's Dynamic Music setting?[/b]
Yes. Some folders fall back to vanilla only when Dynamic Music is enabled, so your choice in the options menu is respected.

[b]Q: Was AI used to make this mod?[/b]

[b][size=5]Third-Party Libraries[/size][/b]

This mod wouldn't be possible without the open-source libraries it's built on. Huge thanks to their authors:
[list]
[*][b]MoonSharp[/b] (BSD 3-Clause + MIT) — Marco Mastropaolo — the embedded Lua interpreter that runs the context.lua decision script. [url=http://www.moonsharp.org/]moonsharp.org[/url]  —  [url=https://github.com/moonsharp-devs/moonsharp]github.com/moonsharp-devs/moonsharp[/url][/*]
[/list]
Audio is decoded and played by a small native library ([i]CMM_Decoder.dll[/i]) bundled with the mod, which compiles in these components:
[list]
[*][b]libopus / libopusfile[/b] (BSD) — Xiph.Org Foundation — Opus decoding. [url=https://github.com/xiph/opusfile]github.com/xiph/opusfile[/url][/*]
[*][b]libvorbis / libvorbisfile[/b] (BSD) — Xiph.Org Foundation — OGG Vorbis decoding. [url=https://github.com/xiph/vorbis]github.com/xiph/vorbis[/url][/*]
[*][b]libogg[/b] (BSD) — Xiph.Org Foundation — Ogg container framing used by Opus and Vorbis. [url=https://github.com/xiph/ogg]github.com/xiph/ogg[/url][/*]
[*][b]dr_flac / dr_mp3 / dr_wav[/b] (public domain) — David Reid (mackron) — FLAC, MP3 and WAV/AIFF decoding. [url=https://github.com/mackron/dr_libs]github.com/mackron/dr_libs[/url][/*]
[*][b]fdk-aac[/b] (Fraunhofer FDK AAC license) — Fraunhofer IIS — AAC-LC / HE-AAC decoding. [url=https://github.com/mstorsjo/fdk-aac]github.com/mstorsjo/fdk-aac[/url][/*]
[*][b]minimp4[/b] (CC0) — Lieff — MP4 / M4A container demuxing. [url=https://github.com/lieff/minimp4]github.com/lieff/minimp4[/url][/*]
[*][b]miniaudio[/b] (public domain) — David Reid (mackron) — cross-platform audio playback. [url=https://github.com/mackron/miniaudio]github.com/mackron/miniaudio[/url][/*]
[/list]
